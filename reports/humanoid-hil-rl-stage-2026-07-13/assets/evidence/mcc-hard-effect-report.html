B1 hard-effect validation v1

Delivered effect

The project now has executable evidence, not only a written Controller contract:

  1. A named-joint TWIST2/MCC composite adapter changes only the fourteen arm joints, preserves all other tracker joints exactly, rate-limits blending, and fails closed on stale, invalid, unmapped or over-force input.
  2. Seven automated tests pass, including exact alpha=0 identity, non-arm invariance, deterministic reset, SONIC LowCmd name mapping, fail-closed behavior and p99 < 20 ms timing.
  3. A locked held-out paired experiment produced complete trajectories and videos for ten TWIST2-only and ten TWIST2+MCC runs.

The strongest supported statement is:

In this fixed-base stationary contact mechanism rig, adding the official MCC admittance/IK core after the released TWIST2 ONNX target preserved the binary task outcome and reduced cumulative palm-panel contact load.

The data do not support the stronger statement that instantaneous contact force was broadly reduced. The conditional F95 improvement is about one percent, while contact-conditioned mean force rises by a median 4.65%; the impulse benefit mainly comes from shorter contact exposure. The minimum duration ratio is 0.6508, only narrowly above the frozen 0.65 floor. The requested force was 5 N while contact F95 stayed near 38 N, so force tracking itself did not pass and is not claimed.

The impulse median has a bootstrap 95% interval of [24.47%, 29.15%], and all ten predefined offsets improve. These offsets span only a narrow fixture perturbation and share one Policy replay, so the statistic is descriptive of this panel, not population-level evidence across independent tasks or Policies.

Scope boundary

This experiment uses the released TWIST2 ONNX checkpoint and real 1432D-to-29D inference at the tracker seam, but it is a mechanism fixture:

  • pelvis welded to world;
  • identical passive arm damping in both branches;
  • recorded-observation replay followed by a held TWIST2 observation;
  • MuJoCo contact truth supplied to the MCC admittance/IK core (F1_gt);
  • a spring-loaded stationary panel, not the final benchmark task.

Therefore it validates the adapter and a Controller effect under correct force input. It does not validate the deployable tau_est wrench route, free-base whole-body behavior, a learned Diffusion Policy, a final benchmark, real robot safety, or actual SONIC integration. Those remain separate gates.

The ten formal seeds represent ten paired ±1 mm fixture offsets. They are held out from development, but they are not ten different task or live-Policy rollouts. The released ONNX executes on the locked 1432D replay input each step; this is a real inference seam test, not a full ReplayDecoupledAgent closed loop.

All 20 original videos are valid, continuous 12-second recordings, but the opaque green panel obscures the upper-body contact region. They prove rollout continuity, not the force effect by visual inspection; the numeric claim comes from the hashed trajectories. A readable transparent-panel rerender is treated only as a visualization derivative of those frozen trajectories.

The delivered readable pair restores every one of the 1,200 recorded states at 100 fps and uses a translucent panel and fixed side camera. Both files have 1,200 unique decoded frames and visibly expose the palm/contact edge. No mj_step, Policy or Controller is executed during rerendering, so the pair is human-facing evidence for the already-hashed trajectory rather than a new run.
