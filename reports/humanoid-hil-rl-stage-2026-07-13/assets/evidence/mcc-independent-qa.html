TWIST2 + MCC formal v1 independent QA

Verdict

  • Preregistered numeric contract: PASS.
  • Archive, trajectories, paired isolation, Controller wiring and automatic criteria: PASS.
  • Original video file integrity: PASS.
  • Original video interpretability: FAIL because the opaque green panel obscures the upper body, right arm and contact region.

The supported result is a scoped fixed-base contact-mechanism result. It is not evidence for a full task, free-base stability, estimated-wrench control, real sensors or real hardware.

1. Archive and data integrity

  • Formal archive size: 52,848,995 bytes.
  • Archive SHA-256: d22a671d2acea21bd4af15bf89d4d8b8e3f7e6cab54aed82a8b269ce8a38e94f.
  • The archive contains 87 files. The manifest registers 86 artifacts; 86/86 exist and all 86/86 SHA-256 values match, with no missing, duplicate or unregistered payload.
  • All 20 formal trajectories contain 1,200 steps, totaling 24,000 steps.
  • A scan of 46,453,085 numeric scalars found zero NaN/Inf values.
  • The local checkpoint hash, HumanoidArena commit, MCC commit, runner hash and adapter hash all match the manifest.

2. Paired A/B isolation

For every paired condition from seed 10 through seed 19, the independent audit confirmed exact equality of:

  • initial condition;
  • 35D task reference;
  • 1,432D observation at every step;
  • TWIST2 raw action at every step;
  • 29D q_track at every step.

In tracker-only runs, q_exec == q_track, and correction and alpha remain zero. In MCC runs, every non-arm joint remains unchanged; only right-arm indices 22–28 change. MCC alpha is active for 147–159 steps per condition and reaches 0.20–0.25. The maximum correction is 0.03647 rad and the maximum step change is 0.00802 rad, within the frozen 0.15 rad and 0.01 rad/step bounds.

The ten conditions share one observation/TWIST2 replay and vary only the panel position across an approximately 1.65 mm range. They are narrow contact geometry perturbations, not ten independent tasks or Policy trajectories.

4. Scientific boundary of “lower contact force”

The primary improvement comes from shorter contact exposure, not a broad reduction of force magnitude while contact is active:

  • median contact-step count decreases by about 30.56%;
  • time-average force and total impulse decrease by a median 27.23%;
  • contact-state F95 decreases by only 0.97%;
  • peak force decreases in 8/10 conditions;
  • contact-conditioned mean force increases by a median of about 4.65%, and increases in all 10/10 conditions;
  • the Controller asks for 5 N, while observed contact-state F95 remains near 38 N; the protocol does not pass 5 N force tracking.

Supported wording:

MCC consistently reduces cumulative contact load across the ten predefined offsets in this fixed fixture and produces a small, consistent reduction in contact-state F95.

Unsupported wording:

MCC has demonstrated stable regulation to the requested force, or a broad reduction in force magnitude throughout active contact.

5. Stability boundary

Paired root and pelvis-tilt deltas are zero, so the automatic stability_noninferior field passes. The pelvis is welded to the world, and MCC does not modify leg commands. This proves only that the fixed-base fixture does not introduce an additional recorded pose delta; it does not prove free-base balance, locomotion stability or real-robot stability.

6. Original video audit

File-level checks pass for all 20/20 original videos:

  • 640×480, 25 fps, 12 s, 300 frames;
  • all 300 decoded frames in every video are distinct;
  • the longest consecutive identical-frame run is one frame.

Visual evidence fails: the green spring panel occupies about 31.07% of the frame and sits over the upper body, right arm and primary contact region. The original videos demonstrate a continuous rollout and fixture state, but do not let a human directly compare the contact behavior. This does not overturn the trajectory evidence. It requires an offline side/rear or transparent-panel rerender from the frozen qpos trajectories; such a rerender must remain a visualization derivative and must not alter the numeric result.

7. Strict scope

The result supports only:

Released TWIST2 ONNX 29D inference-replay seam + official MCC admittance/IK core + named-joint composite adapter, in a fixed-base, passive-damped, stationary spring-panel fixture driven by one-tick-delayed MuJoCo truth wrench in the diagnostic F1gtF1_{gt} branch.

It does not validate live observation feedback after MCC execution, a learned task Policy or Diffusion Policy, the final benchmark, free-base balance, torque-estimated wrench, a real F/T sensor, deployable safety input, real hardware, or continuous 5 N force tracking.

8. Post-audit readable-video derivative

After the original camera failed visual interpretability, a seed-10 A/B pair was rerendered from the frozen trajectories with a fixed side camera and translucent panel. Independent incremental QA confirmed:

  • source trajectory, output video and rerender-script hashes match their manifests;
  • both videos are H.264, 640×480, 100 fps, 12 seconds and 1,200 decoded frames;
  • each has 1,200 distinct frame hashes and no repeated-frame run longer than one frame;
  • at media PTS 10.07 s, corresponding to displayed simulation time 10.08 s, both views expose the upper body, arms, right-palm marker and panel edge; the overlays show the same 38.11 N contact event with alpha 0.000 versus 0.050.

The one-frame offset exists because media frame zero stores simulation time 0.01 s. It is documented rather than hidden. The derivative now passes human-readability QA, but covers only seed 10 and creates no new numeric effect evidence; the locked trajectories and formal manifest remain authoritative. Because the time/force overlay changes every frame, unique frame hashes alone do not prove underlying robot motion; they prove file decodability and distinct complete bitmaps. Motion remains supported by trajectory data and human video inspection.
